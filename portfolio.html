Five artifacts below apply the same instructional-design approach to five different physics ideas: state a single learning objective, build an interactive exhibit that lets the learner manipulate the variable that matters, and close with a question that checks whether the idea transferred.

Artifact 1

From Bulk Materials to Quantum Dots

Learning objective Students will explain how reducing a material from bulk dimensions to nanoscale dimensions changes its electronic energy levels through quantum confinement.

As a semiconductor crystal shrinks from a bulk chunk down toward a single nanometer-scale particle — a quantum dot — the electrons inside it become confined to a smaller and smaller space. Confining a wave to a smaller box forces its allowed energy levels further apart. Move the slider to shrink the particle and watch what happens to the spacing between energy levels.

Artifact 2

Surface-to-Volume Ratio

Learning objective Students will explain why nanoscale materials have a relatively large surface area compared with their volume.

For a cube of edge length s, surface area grows as 6s² while volume grows as s³. Because volume grows faster than surface area as a cube gets bigger, the ratio of surface area to volume gets larger the smaller the cube is. Choose a cube size to see this directly.

Artifact 5

Visual Electrodynamics

Learning objective Students will identify that the electric field, magnetic field, and direction of propagation of an electromagnetic wave are mutually perpendicular.

The solid teal curve represents the electric field oscillating in one plane; the dashed amber curve represents the magnetic field oscillating in the perpendicular plane. Both are perpendicular to the direction the wave travels, shown by the horizontal arrow. Field identity is shown through line style and labels, not color alone. This animation respects your operating system's reduced-motion setting.

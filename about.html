About Me

02

I am an Assistant Professor of Physics at Hawassa University, Ethiopia, where my work sits at the junction of nanophysics, nanotechnology, spectroscopy, and scientific research — and, increasingly, of digital instructional design.

Physics concepts are often difficult to visualize: quantum confinement, electromagnetic-wave propagation, and the relationship between a material's surface area and its volume are easy to state and hard to picture. I build diagrams, animations, simulations, interactive questions, and accessible multimedia explanations because they let a learner test their own understanding against a model, rather than take a description on faith.

This portfolio brings both sides of that work together: the physics I teach and research, and the instructional-design skills I have been developing to teach it better.

Professional interests

  • NxNanophysics and nanotechnology, including green nanotechnology synthesis routes
  • SpSpectroscopy (UV-Vis, FTIR) for material and environmental analysis
  • IdDigital instructional design — active learning, multimedia, accessibility

Physics, visualization, and interactive learning

A diagram that only shows one static state leaves the learner to imagine the variable that actually matters. An interactive model that responds to a slider, a click, or a question lets a learner change that variable themselves and see the physical consequence directly — which is the approach behind every artifact in this portfolio.
